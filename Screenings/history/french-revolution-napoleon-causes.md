# The Corsican Who Ate the Revolution

> How a child of the Revolution became its most brilliant executor—and its final heir.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/french-revolution-napoleon-causes) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/french-revolution-napoleon-causes)

**Related Thinkers**: [[leo-tolstoy|Leo Tolstoy]]

## Core Thesis
The French Revolution did not simply cause Napoleon; it armed him with the vocabulary of merit, the chaos of opportunity, and a nation desperate for order. He was its most gifted student, then its most elegant betrayer. The spoken record—from Josephine's Martinique birth to the Senate's imperial proposal—traces a man who learned revolution's grammar only to write his own coronation. The written scholarship confirms it: Bonaparte was both the Revolution's legacy and its liquidation.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Napoleon's rise was unimaginable without the Revolution's collapse of aristocratic barriers and its promise of careers open to talent.
- His Egyptian campaign—military failure, political gold—shows how he converted revolutionary chaos into personal myth.
- The Senate's offer of empire in 1804 reveals how revolutionary institutions became the machinery of one man's ambition.
- Josephine's story—from Martinique to empress—mirrors the era's dizzying mobility and its fragile, personal costs.
- Napoleon did not want a French Revolution; he wanted a French throne, and the Revolution gave him the ladder.
