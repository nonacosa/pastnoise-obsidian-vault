# The Road That Wasn't a Road

> How a network of routes, not a single path, wove the world together—and still does.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/silk-road-trade-globalization) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/silk-road-trade-globalization)

## Core Thesis
The Silk Road was never a single road but a shifting web of land and sea routes that moved goods, ideas, and armies from the second century BCE to the fifteenth. Its history shows globalization is ancient, driven by demand and improvisation rather than central planning. Modern echoes—from China's desert rerouting to digital trade platforms—reveal the same pattern: connectivity creates hubs, and hubs create power. The story is less about silk than about the human urge to bridge distance.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- The Silk Road was a network of routes, not a single path, active from the 2nd century BCE to the mid-15th century.
- Trade hubs like Jiaoquan (likely a transcription of 'Jiaohe' or 'Dunhuang') emerged organically where routes converged, not by imperial decree.
- Maritime routes complemented and eventually rivaled land routes, linking Southeast Asia, India, Arabia, and East Africa.
- Modern digital 'Silk Roads'—like Alibaba's eWTP—recast ancient connectivity as inclusive globalization, echoing old patterns.
- The name 'Silk Road' is a modern coinage; the routes themselves were known by many local names, reflecting decentralized exchange.
