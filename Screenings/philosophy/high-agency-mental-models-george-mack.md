# The Map Before the Territory

> Mental models are not ornaments of thought but the load-bearing walls of attention and action.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/high-agency-mental-models-george-mack) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/high-agency-mental-models-george-mack)

## Core Thesis
Across these conversations, mental models emerge less as clever frameworks than as the invisible architecture of perception. Bill Gurley asks which models repay repeated use; Stewart Butterfield shows a model tested at Slack’s launch; Ed Thorp treats models as instruments for decisions under uncertainty; Charles Duhigg links focus to deliberate construction of attention. Cognitive science and Laing's psychiatry remind us that minds are modeling engines, and that models can clarify or distort. The practical task is not collecting models but auditing and rebuilding them.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- The value of a model is not its elegance but its repeated usefulness under real uncertainty, as Gurley's question and Thorp's practice imply.
- Slack's 2014 launch illustrates that product models must survive contact with users; introspection is a poor substitute for observed behavior and iteration.
- Duhigg's focus problem is environmental and cognitive: attention is built by structuring cues, not by willing concentration against a vibrating pocket.
- Cognitive science treats the mind as an active model-builder, so bad outcomes often trace to bad maps rather than mere laziness or lack of effort.
- Laing and Models for Mental Disorder caution that models of mind carry ethical weight: they can illuminate suffering or become instruments of control.
