# The Stillness Beneath the Noise

> What meditation reveals about attention, self, and the depths of consciousness.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/meditation-focus-consciousness) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/meditation-focus-consciousness)

## Core Thesis
Meditation is not a retreat from consciousness but a refinement of it. Huberman and Harris frame it as attentional training that reveals the constructed nature of self, while Seppälä grounds it in the cost of mind-wandering. Blackmore and Pollan push further, questioning who is even watching. Chopra and the consciousness documentary return us to stillness as a doorway—not an escape—where inquiry into purpose and awareness becomes a practical, daily act.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Mind-wandering occupies roughly half of waking life and correlates with unhappiness; meditation trains the return to presence.
- Meditation is attention training that detaches from discursive thought, not a relaxation technique or mystical escape.
- The sense of a fixed self may be an illusion; meditation and neuroscience both suggest consciousness is a process, not a thing.
- Stillness is not emptiness but a deeper layer of awareness, accessible through self-inquiry and sustained practice.
- Psychedelics and meditation converge on the same question: what remains when the narrative self quiets?
