# The Engine of Everything

> Jensen Huang's GPU revolution is less about chips than about the patience to see what others missed.

## Core Thesis
Jensen Huang's story is not one of sudden genius but of stubborn conviction: that graphics chips could become the engine of artificial intelligence. Through decades of quiet engineering and a refusal to abandon the long view, NVIDIA turned a niche gaming technology into the infrastructure of a new era. The spoken excerpts reveal a mind obsessed with latency, learning, and the psychology of persistence—while the written record confirms a company that became indispensable. This is a portrait of how deep technical foresight, married to relentless execution, reshapes the world.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Huang's early bet on GPU computing was not a single insight but a sustained willingness to be misunderstood for years.
- The AI revolution is less about raw power than about reducing latency—making intelligence feel instantaneous and natural.
- NVIDIA's dominance rests on a culture that treats textbooks and first principles as competitive advantages, not nostalgia.
- Personalization at scale, from drug discovery to customer service, is the practical horizon Huang has been building toward.
- The 'AI Oppenheimer' label misses the point: Huang frames AI as processing data, not wielding godlike power.
