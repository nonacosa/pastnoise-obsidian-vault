# The Ground Beneath the Pixels

> Fei-Fei Li's spatial intelligence asks machines to see where things are—and what they mean.

## Core Thesis
Fei-Fei Li's work moves from labeling images to grounding them: ImageNet taught machines to recognize, but spatial intelligence asks them to perceive, reason, and act in three dimensions. Her claim is not that AI has arrived but that the hard step—generating pixels with spatial context—is now underway. Against triumphalism, she keeps science humble and generational judgment suspicious. The dossier reads that discipline as a practical ethic: build systems that understand the real world, and remain curious enough to know what they still cannot see.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- ImageNet proved recognition can be scaled; spatial intelligence asks whether machines can locate objects, causes, and consequences in a shared physical world.
- Li treats science as inquiry rather than revelation: its authority comes from correction, not from pretending present methods have captured the whole truth.
- Her TED demonstration—showing 'nothing' from 540 million years ago—frames sight as an evolutionary bargain, not a default human power.
- The generational complaint is a recurring blind spot; Li suggests curiosity across generations is more productive than lamenting the future's supposed ignorance.
- Spatial grounding in AI is not cosmetic realism; it is the difference between describing an image and understanding where things are, how they move, and what can happen next.
