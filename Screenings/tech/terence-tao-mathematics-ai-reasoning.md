# The Jam Sandwich and the Proof Machine

> Terence Tao, Lean, and the quiet collision of human intuition with machine-checked proof.

## Core Thesis
Tao’s voice keeps returning mathematics to the human scale: a jam sandwich before Numberphile, a parallel line that never crosses, almost-primes understood better than primes. Yet the written record shows the field moving toward mechanized certainty—Lean’s type theory, language models trained on proof, finite algebraic structures searched by machine. The synthesis is not replacement but calibration: intuition proposes, formal systems dispose, and the documentary listens for what either side cannot say alone. The result is a more honest account of discovery: slower, stranger, and more collaborative than the eureka myth allows.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Tao’s casual excerpts resist the eureka myth: mathematics grows from ordinary attention, parallel lines, jam sandwiches, and almost-primes rather than sudden lightning.
- Lean matters because it turns proof into an object a machine can audit, shifting trust from charismatic authority toward reproducible formal checking.
- Language-model work such as DeepSeekMath and Nature’s program-search paper suggests search and proof are converging, but citation counts measure attention, not mathematical truth.
- The BKT projection theorem reminds us that technical progress often hinges on subfields and structure—small constraints that make a wild problem tractable.
- Automated theorem proving in finite algebraic structures is less glamorous than AGI, yet it is where machine assistance can quietly become routine.
