# The Quiet Factory at the Center of the World

> How a Taiwanese foundry became the hinge of geopolitics, and why the supply chain is a human story.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/semiconductor-supply-chain-geopolitics) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/semiconductor-supply-chain-geopolitics)

## Core Thesis
The semiconductor supply chain is not a diagram but a cascade of human decisions—Intel's CEO notes that even funded startups change their plans halfway. Bloomberg shows the cleanroom's fragile choreography; Micro reminds us that most chips are mundane, not martial. Yet China's drive for self-sufficiency and Taiwan's singular role at TSMC turn ordinary manufacturing into a geopolitical fault line. The literature confirms: complexity is the real vulnerability, and resilience begins with seeing the whole.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Supply chains are living systems: business plans shift mid-course, so fixed maps mislead more than they guide.
- The cleanest factories depend on the messiest geopolitics—Taiwan's TSMC is both marvel and single point of failure.
- Most chips are not AI or fighter jets; the quiet majority of production still anchors modern economies.
- China's push for self-sufficiency is a response to dependency, not a rejection of global trade.
- Peer-reviewed work links supply chain resilience directly to geopolitical literacy, not just logistics.
