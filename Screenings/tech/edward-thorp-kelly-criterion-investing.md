# The Bet That Keeps You Alive

> Kelly sizing is not a forecast; it is a discipline for surviving your own certainty.

## Core Thesis
Kelly criterion is an old answer to a permanent human problem: how to wager when the future is partly unknowable. By maximizing expected log wealth, it turns position size into a survival rule, not a prophecy. Shiller's insurance archetype, Dicle's option warning, and the retail futures conversation all converge on the same practical grammar: protection, leverage, and ruin are sized decisions. The Kelly discipline asks not whether you are right, but whether you can stay in the game long enough to be right.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Kelly sizing treats ruin as asymmetric: a single zero can erase decades of edge, so bet size must protect compounding before it chases return.
- Insurance and options are cousins: both price uncertainty, but options can flip from protection to leveraged loss far beyond premium paid.
- The digital twin remains aspirational in risk management; no known financial institution has built it, so judgment and guardrails still carry the load.
- Retail futures education begins with mechanics but ends with risk management, because leverage turns small errors into account-level events.
- Maximizing log wealth is not maximizing every trade's expected value; it is optimizing the sequence that determines whether you survive.
