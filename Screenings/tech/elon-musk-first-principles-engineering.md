# The Cost of the Possible

> First principles is Musk’s method for pricing the future against physics, capital, and power.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/elon-musk-first-principles-engineering) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/elon-musk-first-principles-engineering)

## Core Thesis
Elon Musk’s first principles are often mistaken for a slogan. In practice they are an accounting discipline: strip a rocket, battery, or factory to physics, then ask which prices are inherited rather than necessary. Kaku’s warning about Apollo’s unsustainable five-percent budget frames the wager; Starfactory and solar panels show where arithmetic meets execution; trillionaire politics and AI ethics show where private capability becomes public consequence. The written record—Musk’s physics background, AI literacy research, ethics guidelines—sharpens the point: method without governance is merely velocity.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- First principles turns ambition into a bill of materials: question inherited prices, then rebuild rockets, batteries, and factories from physics outward.
- Kaku’s five-percent NASA budget shows Apollo was politically magnificent but fiscally unsustainable; Musk’s wager is reuse, private capital, and production tempo.
- The trillionaire debate is not mere envy; it asks whether concentrated private power can be trusted with public infrastructure, artificial intelligence, and space.
- Musk’s physics training matters less as credential than as habit: decompose problems, tolerate uncomfortable arithmetic, then accept execution risk in public.
- AI literacy and ethics guidelines converge on one point: capability without governance is not first principles but first improvisation.
