# A Phone Call, a Foundry, a Bottleneck

> Morris Chang made contract manufacturing—and one island—the hinge of global computation.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/tsmc-morris-chang-semiconductor-empire) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/tsmc-morris-chang-semiconductor-empire)

## Core Thesis
Morris Chang’s life shows how institutional courage becomes infrastructure. Texas Instruments taught him scale; Taiwan’s invitation gave him a country-sized bet; TSMC turned pure-play foundry into a neutrality that designers could trust. Jensen Huang’s remembered phone call and Apple’s supplier decisions are not anecdotes but symptoms: when manufacturing capacity concentrates, diplomacy shrinks to a purchase order. The written record—from transnational production networks to energy-hungry AI systems—confirms the same lesson. A single company on a single island is both a marvel of specialization and a standing invitation to strategic anxiety.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Chang’s foundry model converted neutrality into capacity: designers everywhere could compete without owning fabs, while TSMC absorbed the capital risk.
- The bottleneck is not merely technical but geopolitical: AI’s appetite meets one company’s manufacturing concentration on one island.
- Jensen Huang’s anecdote about Morris Chang’s call dramatizes how personal trust can precede industrial dependency, then harden into market structure.
- Chang’s departure from Texas Instruments and Taiwan’s recruitment show careers as infrastructure decisions—talent migration can redirect national fortunes.
- CNBC’s note that early investors hesitated reminds us that the obvious system was once an unlikely proposal requiring patient institutional backing.
