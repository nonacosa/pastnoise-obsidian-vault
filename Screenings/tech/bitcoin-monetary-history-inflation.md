# The Ledger and the Bleeding

> What Bitcoin's short history reveals about inflation, trust, and the stories we tell about money.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/bitcoin-monetary-history-inflation) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/bitcoin-monetary-history-inflation)

**Related Thinkers**: [[warren-buffett-charlie-munger|Warren Buffett & Charlie Munger]]

## Core Thesis
Bitcoin's monetary history is not a simple inflation hedge but a mirror of our faith in institutions. Balaji Srinivasan admits the charts don't yet show the hedge; Michael Saylor invokes George Washington's death by well-meaning physicians as a parable of misguided intervention. Meanwhile, Bernholz's history of monetary regimes shows inflation is a recurring human drama, not a technical glitch. The tension between Bitcoin's sound-money aspiration and its volatile reality exposes how we confuse narrative with evidence.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Bitcoin has not yet reliably acted as an inflation hedge, as Balaji Srinivasan concedes—its short history lacks the data to prove the thesis.
- Michael Saylor's analogy of Washington's physicians warns that well-intentioned monetary policy can bleed an economy dry.
- Bernholz's study of metallic regimes shows inflation predates fiat money, challenging the idea that Bitcoin alone solves monetary debasement.
- The Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and El Salvador's adoption signal state-level experimentation, but their outcomes remain uncertain.
- Misinformation about inflation—treating it as purely evil or deflation as ideal—obscures the trade-offs central banks actually face.
