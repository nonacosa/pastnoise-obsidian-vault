## Core Thesis
The longevity conversation around vitamin D and omega-3s is less a hunt for a miracle capsule than a study in compounding restraint. Rhonda Patrick's excitement over a graph and Physionic's caution about nuance belong together: trials hint at slower aging, not immortality. Ergothioneine and multivitamin memory claims widen the frame, reminding us that food patterns, exercise, and baseline deficiency matter. DO-HEALTH lends the written record its sober spine. The practical worldview is unglamorous—correct what is missing, test rather than guess, and let small biological negotiations accumulate.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- The DO-HEALTH trial frames vitamin D3, omega-3s, and exercise as combinable levers, but its design promises nuance, not a single anti-aging switch.
- Rhonda Patrick's 'mind-blowing' graph is an invitation to read mechanisms closely; Physionic's hesitation reminds us that association and slowed aging are not identical.
- Ergothioneine appears in commentary as a 'longevity vitamin,' yet food-source epidemiology and supplement marketing should not be collapsed into one claim.
- Multivitamin memory findings and 'normal labs' narratives point to baseline deficiency and measurement limits more than universal supplementation.
- The practical takeaway is unromantic: fix documented deficiencies, eat whole foods, move, and resist turning every biomarker into a product.
