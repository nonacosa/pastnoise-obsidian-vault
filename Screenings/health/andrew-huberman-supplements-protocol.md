## Core Thesis
Magnesium arrives in the conversation wearing two faces: a bedtime ally and an overpromised mineral. Huberman is openly bullish before sleep, then cautions that many already get enough; a doctor calls it no magic fix; the indexed literature asks which preparation is correct. The dossier’s worldview is practical and humane: supplements belong inside sleep, diet, digestion, training, and commerce—not above them. The work is to listen closely, measure honestly, and refuse both blind enthusiasm and cynical dismissal.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Huberman is bullish on magnesium before sleep, yet he also warns that many people already get enough from food.
- A doctor’s commentary calls magnesium no magic fix; digestive issues and individual context decide whether supplementing helps at all.
- The indexed question “What is the correct magnesium supplement?” shifts attention from hype to preparation, absorption, and dose.
- Galpin and Huberman place magnesium among fasting, training, salt, and potassium, reminding us that protocols are systems, not single pills.
- The AGZ mention makes commerce visible inside a personal sleep regimen, a useful reminder to separate anecdote from recommendation.
