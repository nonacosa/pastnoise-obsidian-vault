# The Clock We Carry

> Sleep is a daily negotiation between inner time, outer light, and the pressure of being awake.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/sleep-architecture-circadian-recovery) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/sleep-architecture-circadian-recovery)

## Core Thesis
Sleep is not an isolated collapse but a timed negotiation: a circadian oscillation near twenty-four hours, a sleep drive that accumulates, and the day's light, meals, movement, and stress pressing upon both. The speakers converge on a practical claim—regularity is physiology, not moral decoration—while the literature names what happens when timing fails: circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders, misalignment, and metabolic cost. The documentary's deeper argument is humane: to sleep well is to live in better time with the world, and to treat rhythm as a form of care rather than a personal virtue.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Circadian rhythms are endogenous oscillations near twenty-four hours; they time sleep, metabolism, and arousal, and they can be disrupted by modern schedules.
- Sleep is jointly governed by circadian rhythm and sleep drive, so timing and pressure matter more than a single heroic bedtime.
- Circadian-timed eating and exercise can support metabolic health, but the evidence points to consistency and alignment, not clock-watching perfection.
- Misalignment and insufficient sleep are linked to obesity and metabolic strain; the risk is not just short sleep but sleep at the wrong biological hour.
- Light is the master anchor: morning daylight stabilizes rhythm, while late-night light and irregular meals blur the body's internal noon.
