## Core Thesis
Bryan Johnson’s longevity project is less a single protocol than a wager: that measurement, repetition, and public accountability can make mortality negotiable. His five habits and 130 grams of protein sit beside warnings that biology resists command, and his own caution that uncontrolled drugs need protocols. Critics ask whether avoiding death is the highest good; defenders answer with dashboards, not sermons. The dossier’s tension is practical and philosophical: biomarkers can clarify risk, but they cannot decide what a life is for.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Johnson’s public method converts longevity from private hope into measurable routine: five habits, daily protein targets, and repeated biomarker checks.
- Physionic’s Nature study warning frames the central limit: extending life expectancy is biologically hard, not merely a matter of better adherence.
- Bryan’s ketamine caution—uncontrolled drugs require protocols—reveals a conservative experimental ethic inside an otherwise radical self-optimization project.
- Twobilee reframes success as positive existence and reduced suffering, challenging longevity culture’s implicit priority of avoiding death over living well.
- The New Scientist Olympian phrase and AHA’s Essential 8 show elite discipline borrowing clinical benchmarks while still lacking proof of lifespan payoff.
