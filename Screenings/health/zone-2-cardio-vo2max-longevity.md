## Core Thesis
VO2 max is not just a number for athletes; it is a vital sign of how efficiently the body converts air into life. The conversation around zone 2 cardio and high-intensity intervals reveals a practical path: most of us are far from our ceiling, and the first gains come from simply moving more. Yet the science also warns against reducing longevity to a single metric. The real work is building a sustainable practice that respects both the heart's need for gentle volume and its appetite for occasional challenge.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- VO2 max is a strong predictor of longevity, but it is a proxy for systemic health, not a magic number to chase.
- Zone 2 training builds the aerobic base that makes high-intensity work possible and sustainable over decades.
- Even self-described non-athletes can improve VO2 max significantly with consistent, time-efficient protocols.
- The biggest gains come from moving from sedentary to moderately active, not from optimizing an already elite engine.
- Exercise is the most powerful longevity drug we have, but it works best when tailored to individual capacity and enjoyment.
