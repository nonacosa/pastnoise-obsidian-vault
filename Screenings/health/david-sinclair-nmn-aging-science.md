# The Molecule and the Mortality Clause

> David Sinclair's longevity science asks what we will do with more time—and who gets to buy it.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/david-sinclair-nmn-aging-science) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/david-sinclair-nmn-aging-science)

**Related Thinkers**: [[david-sinclair|Dr. David Sinclair]]

## Core Thesis
Sinclair frames aging as a disease-like process of lost cellular information, not an unbreakable clock. The spoken record pushes from podcast welcome to glaucoma, from a possible 120-year ceiling to Lex Fridman's question about non-existence. The written record supplies mechanism—declining NAD+, disrupted nuclear-mitochondrial communication, autophagy, sirtuin-activating compounds—but not proof that NMN or resveratrol extends human life. PastNoise's synthesis: longevity is plastic enough to study, commercial enough to distort, and existential enough that evidence and meaning must travel together.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Sinclair treats aging as addressable biology, not an unbreakable clock; that reframing makes glaucoma and other diseases legitimate longevity targets.
- NAD+ decline and impaired nuclear-mitochondrial talk are real age-linked mechanisms; supplement claims must still clear human-outcome trials, not just mouse plausibility.
- Resveratrol and sirtuin activators are caloric-restriction mimetics in concept; the gap between pathway activity and lived lifespan is where marketing rushes in.
- The 120-year question is less about biology's ceiling than about meaning: Sinclair's limit talk collides with Lex's question about non-existence.
- For parents, the practical question is not molecule-of-the-month but sleep, exercise, diet, risk, and supervised evidence—HN's thread names the stakes.
