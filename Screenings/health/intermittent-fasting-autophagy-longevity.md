# The Quiet Hours Between Meals

> What fasting really does inside the body—and why the science is both promising and provisional.

**Watch with synced bilingual subtitles**: [English Screening Room](https://pastnoise.com/film/intermittent-fasting-autophagy-longevity) | [中文双语放映室](https://pastnoise.com/zh/film/intermittent-fasting-autophagy-longevity)

## Core Thesis
Intermittent fasting is less a diet than a rhythm—a deliberate pause that lets the body repair what daily eating leaves unattended. The spoken voices here, from clinicians to longevity researchers, converge on autophagy as the mechanism, while the written record grounds it in spermidine, gene expression, and lifespan studies. Yet the evidence is often indirect, drawn from mice and markers, not from decades of human lives. The honest takeaway: fasting is a plausible lever for healthspan, not a proven elixir.

## 30-Second Takeaways
- Autophagy is the cell's recycling program, and fasting appears to flip its switch—but most human evidence relies on indirect markers, not direct measurement.
- Spermidine is essential for fasting-mediated autophagy, linking a polyamine to longevity and suggesting that fasting works through specific molecular keys.
- Ramadan dawn-to-dusk fasting increased autophagy-related gene expression in humans, offering rare human data for a practice long studied in rodents.
- Fasting plus exercise may synergize to preserve cardiomyocytes and enhance autophagy, pointing to combination strategies over fasting alone.
- In mice, intermittent fasting extended lifespan even beyond a longevity diet, but the gap between mouse and human biology remains wide and unresolved.
